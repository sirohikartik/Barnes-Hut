\begin{abstract}
Denoising diffusion models approximate physical dynamical systems. Autoregressive rollouts with generative surrogates frequently deviate from physical manifolds. This paper presents an empirical assessment of conditional diffusion surrogates trained on pseudo-Newtonian Barnes-Hut $N$-body systems. The physical setup evaluates non-plunging trajectories outside the innermost stable circular orbit. Dataset partitioning enforces strict trajectory-level separation across five independent random seeds. We benchmark four denoiser widths against matched deterministic multi-layer perceptrons, a constant-velocity kinematic baseline, and a zero-motion identity baseline.

The evaluation demonstrates a clear regime transition across training data volume. A single fixed held-out evaluation set of 15 trajectories establishes stable benchmarks. Non-parametric kinematic ($2.86$, $95\%$ CI: $[2.48, 3.22]$) and zero-motion identity ($2.94$, $95\%$ CI: $[2.56, 3.29]$) baselines outperform all learned surrogates on small training sets ($1,475$ transitions). Scaling training data to $14,750$ transitions enables deterministic regressors to reach $1.80$ ($95\%$ CI: $[1.58, 2.03]$), surpassing both non-parametric baselines. Diffusion models improve single-step validation loss ($0.862 \to 0.557$ for $d=64$, $0.751 \to 0.246$ for $d=128$), yet recursive rollouts suffer compounding divergence. Deterministic DDIM sampling contracts inflated step displacement norms from $8.21\times$ to $7.15\times$, cutting rollout error ($13.39 \to 10.23$ for $d=64$). As training data expands, DDIM advantage narrows, and 5-sample averaging ($4.59$ for $d=128$) matches or surpasses DDIM ($4.83$). Multi-sample averaging isolates systematic transition error. Physical state features strongly predict this denoised error ($R^2 = 0.685, r = 0.878$). Single-sample stochastic error remains dominated by sampling noise ($R^2 = 0.151$). Internal network activations yield negative test $R^2$ scores across all error and invariant probes. Matched random-weight networks show equivalent negative scores. Exact coordinates also fail under linear Ridge probes ($R^2 = -3.39$ for energy). Linear diagnostic probing cannot distinguish whether representations encode physical invariants.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Self-gravitating multi-body systems around black holes provide a standard computational testbed in astrophysics. Direct summation requires $O(N^2)$ pairwise operations per timestep. Spatial octree algorithms reduce this computational load. The 3D Barnes-Hut tree \cite{Barnes1986} groups distant particles into multipole expansions, scaling as $O(N \log N)$. Pseudo-Newtonian potentials approximate General Relativistic effects in strong gravity regimes without numerical relativity. The Paczy\'nski--Wiita potential \cite{Paczynski1980} reproduces the Schwarzschild event horizon at $r_s = 2GM/c^2$ and the innermost stable circular orbit at $r_{\mathrm{ISCO}} = 3r_s$.

Numerical integrators evaluate these equations sequentially. Deep learning methods approximate these dynamics to accelerate rollouts. Graph neural networks \cite{sanchez-gonzalez2020learning, battaglia2016interaction} map particle interactions using relational graphs. Conditional denoising diffusion probabilistic models \cite{ho2020denoising, song2021scorebased} represent continuous dynamical fields in weather prediction and fluid simulation \cite{finn2024representation, li2024generative, bastek2024physics}. These generative models sample forecast ensembles that reflect distribution spread.

Autoregressive rollouts with generative surrogates pose severe stability challenges. Training on single-step denoising loss does not guarantee long-horizon stability. Rollouts leave conservative energy surfaces and diverge. Studies in neural partial differential equations address this limitation using pushforward objectives \cite{brandstetter2022message} and iterative score refinement \cite{LippeVPTB23}. Three technical questions arise for pseudo-Newtonian multi-body systems:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Capacity and Rollout Dynamics:} Denoiser capacity alters single-step denoising loss. Recursive stability requires verification against simple non-parametric baselines across data scales.
    \item \textbf{Representation Grounding:} Linear decodability of physical invariants across hidden widths requires validation against positive controls and random networks.
    \item \textbf{Error Predictability and Runtime Intervention:} Internal activations must be compared against state difficulty metrics to verify predictive error signals.
\end{enumerate}

This study establishes a controlled benchmark for conditional diffusion surrogates in pseudo-Newtonian $N$-body mechanics. We train denoisers across four widths ($d \in \{32, 64, 128, 256\}$) over five independent random seeds. The physical system evaluates $N=16$ self-gravitating bodies orbiting outside the innermost stable circular orbit ($r \ge 2.5 \approx 4.5 r_s$). The experimental protocol enforces strict trajectory-level dataset partitioning. Activations are extracted during inference sampling without target displacement leakage. We benchmark diffusion rollouts against matched deterministic regressors, a first-order kinematic baseline, and a zero-motion identity baseline on a dedicated held-out set.

This paper presents five empirical findings:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Regime Transition Across Data Scales:} Small datasets (1,475 transitions) show kinematic ($2.86$) and identity ($2.94$) baselines outperforming all learned models. Tenfold data scaling enables deterministic regressors to reach $1.80 \pm 0.11$. This performance surpasses both non-parametric baselines.
    \item \textbf{Deterministic DDIM Stabilization and Shrinkage:} Deterministic DDIM sampling ($\eta = 0$) \cite{song2020denoising} eliminates per-step Gaussian noise. DDIM contracts step displacement inflation from $8.21\times$ to $7.15\times$, reducing small-data rollout error from $13.39$ to $10.23$. Scaling training data narrows DDIM advantage, allowing 5-sample averaging ($4.59$) to surpass DDIM ($4.83$).
    \item \textbf{Control-Calibrated Invariant Probing:} Cross-validated Ridge probes decode linear coordinate targets with $R^2 \ge 0.998$. Linear probes yield negative $R^2$ on quadratic invariants ($R^2 = -3.39$ for energy) even from exact coordinates. Linear probing cannot determine whether internal representations encode invariants.
    \item \textbf{Denoised Error Predictability:} Denoised transition errors correlate strongly with physical state features ($R^2 = 0.685, r = 0.878$). Internal activations achieve negative test $R^2$ scores across all layers.
    \item \textbf{Intervention Mechanism Analysis:} Multi-seed runtime interventions demonstrate that constant scalar damping ($9.88 \pm 0.63$) matches or outperforms probe-guided damping ($10.28 \pm 0.38$). Rollout stabilization stems from trajectory regularization toward stationary states rather than predictive probe guidance.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related_work}

Hierarchical algorithms accelerate multi-body gravitational calculations. Barnes and Hut \cite{Barnes1986} replaced distant particle clusters with multipole approximations, cutting computation from $O(N^2)$ to $O(N \log N)$. The Paczy\'nski--Wiita potential \cite{Paczynski1980} models Schwarzschild orbital phenomena, capturing the event horizon and the innermost stable circular orbit. Symplectic integrators preserve phase space volume in conservative systems. Velocity-Verlet integration \cite{Verlet1967} provides second-order accuracy with bounded energy drift. Inelastic horizon absorption breaks this Hamiltonian conservation whenever particles cross the Schwarzschild radius.

Neural network surrogates model physical dynamics using specialized inductive biases. Graph Network Simulators \cite{sanchez-gonzalez2020learning, battaglia2016interaction} approximate continuum and particle dynamics via relational graph message passing. Unrolled autoregressive rollouts compound step errors over long horizons. Brandstetter et al. \cite{brandstetter2022message} introduced pushforward training objectives that unroll solver steps and inject state perturbations to mitigate autoregressive error compounding in neural PDE solvers. Symplectic neural architectures embed exact conservation laws. Hamiltonian Neural Networks \cite{greydanus2019hamiltonian} and Lagrangian Neural Networks \cite{cranmer2020lagrangian} parameterize dynamics via continuous gradients. These formulations require smooth conservation surfaces, restricting them to conservative Hamiltonian systems.

Generative diffusion models approximate complex dynamical systems. Denoising diffusion probabilistic models \cite{ho2020denoising} and continuous score-based formulations \cite{song2021scorebased} represent data distributions by inverting gradual noise corruption. Finn et al. \cite{finn2024representation} showed that unconditional diffusion models capture low-dimensional dynamical manifolds. Li et al. \cite{li2024generative} deployed conditional diffusion models for operational ensemble weather forecasting. Bastek et al. \cite{bastek2024physics} penalized differential equation residuals to guide outputs toward physical consistency. Lippe et al. \cite{LippeVPTB23} introduced PDE-Refiner, refining neural solver rollouts with score-based steps. Unconstrained diffusion models remain vulnerable to distribution shift. Rozet et al. \cite{rozet2025lost} demonstrated that latent diffusion surrogates drift from physical manifolds during recursive rollouts.

Diagnostic probing assesses representations inside deep networks. Alain and Bengio \cite{Alain2016UnderstandingIL} introduced linear probes to monitor feature decodability across network layers. Belinkov \cite{belinkov-2022-probing} reviewed diagnostic probing, noting that probes often capture superficial dataset correlations. Kim et al. \cite{kim2018interpretability} defined Concept Activation Vectors to measure human-interpretable directions in activation spaces. Zhang and Nanda \cite{zhang2024best} applied activation patching to isolate functional pathways in transformer representations. Geiger et al. \cite{geiger2023causal} developed causal abstraction frameworks to link symbolic variables with latent states. We apply diagnostic probing protocols to conditional diffusion surrogates. We benchmark representations against random-weight controls and non-parametric baselines.

\section{System Methodology}

\subsection{Ground Truth Pseudo-Newtonian \texorpdfstring{$N$}{N}-Body Simulation}
A C++17 simulation engine computes ground truth trajectories for $N=16$ particles orbiting a central black hole of mass $M_{\mathrm{BH}}$. The equations of motion combine central black hole gravity with softened pairwise self-gravity.

The central black hole exerts gravitational acceleration through the Paczy\'nski--Wiita potential \cite{Paczynski1980}:
\begin{equation}
    \Phi_{\mathrm{PW}}(r) = -\frac{G M_{\mathrm{BH}}}{r - r_s}, \qquad \vec{a}_{\mathrm{PW}}(r) = -\frac{G M_{\mathrm{BH}}}{(r - r_s)^2} \frac{\vec{r}}{r}, \quad (r > r_s),
\end{equation}
where $r = \|\vec{r}\|$ represents radial distance, and $r_s$ denotes the Schwarzschild radius:
\begin{equation}
    r_s = \frac{2 G M_{\mathrm{BH}}}{c^2}.
\end{equation}
The Paczy\'nski--Wiita field models two relativistic landmarks: the event horizon at $r_s$ and the innermost stable circular orbit at $r_{\mathrm{ISCO}} = 3.0 r_s$. Unstable circular trajectories inside $r_{\mathrm{ISCO}}$ plunge inward toward the central singularity. Our experimental setup initializes particles outside this plunging regime ($r \ge 2.5 \approx 4.5 r_s$), ensuring stable continuous orbital evaluation.

Orbiting particles interact through self-gravity via a 3D Barnes-Hut octree \cite{Barnes1986}. An octree cell with width $s$ and center of mass $\vec{R}_{\mathrm{com}}$ satisfies the acceptance criterion relative to body $\vec{r}_i$ when:
\begin{equation}
    \frac{s}{\|\vec{R}_{\mathrm{com}} - \vec{r}_i\|} < \theta,
\end{equation}
with opening angle $\theta = 0.6$. The system contains $N=16$ bodies, serving as an interpretable multi-body benchmark. Pairwise forces incorporate Plummer softening \cite{Plummer1911} with radius $\epsilon = 0.08$ to prevent numerical divergences:
\begin{equation}
    \vec{a}_{ij} = \frac{G m_j (\vec{r}_j - \vec{r}_i)}{\left(\|\vec{r}_j - \vec{r}_i\|^2 + \epsilon^2\right)^{3/2}}.
\end{equation}

The second-order Velocity-Verlet algorithm \cite{Verlet1967} advances the orbital state:
\begin{align}
    \vec{x}(t + \Delta t) &= \vec{x}(t) + \vec{v}(t)\Delta t + \frac{1}{2}\vec{a}(t)\Delta t^2, \\
    \vec{v}(t + \Delta t) &= \vec{v}(t) + \frac{1}{2}\Big(\vec{a}(t) + \vec{a}(t + \Delta t)\Big)\Delta t.
\end{align}
Particles crossing the Schwarzschild event horizon ($r \le r_s$) undergo inelastic accretion. The particle deactivates immediately. Its mass and momentum transfer to the central black hole:
\begin{equation}
    M_{\mathrm{BH}} \leftarrow M_{\mathrm{BH}} + m_i, \qquad \vec{P}_{\mathrm{BH}} \leftarrow \vec{P}_{\mathrm{BH}} + m_i \vec{v}_i.
\end{equation}
The black hole updates its Schwarzschild radius:
\begin{equation}
    r_s \leftarrow \frac{2 G M_{\mathrm{BH}}}{c^2}.
\end{equation}
Accretion events terminate particle orbits, breaking Hamiltonian conservation in strong fields.

\subsection{Conditional Diffusion Surrogate}
The physical state vector $x_t \in \mathbb{R}^D$ concatenates 3D coordinates and velocities for all $N$ particles and the central black hole:
\begin{equation}
    x_t = [\mathbf{p}_1, \mathbf{v}_1, \dots, \mathbf{p}_N, \mathbf{v}_N, \mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{BH}}, \mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{BH}}]^T \in \mathbb{R}^{6N + 6}.
\end{equation}
With $N=16$, the state dimension equals $D=102$. The surrogate predicts the normalized state displacement vector $\Delta x_t = x_{t+1} - x_t$.

The forward diffusion process corrupts target displacement $y_0 = \Delta x_t$ across $K=20$ discrete steps. We apply a cosine schedule \cite{nichol2021improved} with variance clipping:
\begin{equation}
    \bar{\alpha}_k = \frac{f(k)}{f(0)}, \quad f(k) = \cos^2\left(\frac{k/K + s}{1 + s} \frac{\pi}{2}\right),
\end{equation}
using offset $s = 0.008$. Individual variances satisfy $\beta_k = \min(1 - \bar{\alpha}_k / \bar{\alpha}_{k-1}, 0.1)$. This clipping bounds $1/\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_K}$ and avoids numerical instability during reverse sampling.

The denoiser $\epsilon_\theta(y_k, k, x_t)$ parameterizes noise predictions with width $d$. We evaluate widths $d \in \{32, 64, 128, 256\}$. The conditioning state $x_t$ is normalized using training statistics with variance floors ($\sigma_{\min} \ge 0.05$) to prevent static coordinates from inflating numerical noise. The architecture combines an input projection layer, three residual blocks with LayerNorm and GELU activations, and a linear output layer. Training minimizes the mean squared error loss:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{k, y_0, \epsilon}\left[\|\epsilon - \epsilon_\theta(y_k, k, x_t)\|^2\right], \quad \epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \mathbf{I}).
\end{equation}

Ancestral reverse sampling generates predicted displacements from Gaussian noise $y_K \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \mathbf{I})$:
\begin{equation}
    y_{k-1} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_k}}\left(y_k - \frac{\beta_k}{\sqrt{1 - \bar{\alpha}_k}}\epsilon_\theta(y_k, k, x_t)\right) + \sigma_k \mathbf{z},
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{z} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \mathbf{I})$ for $k > 1$ and $\mathbf{z} = 0$ at $k=1$. Estimated $x_0$ predictions are clamped to $[-3.5, 3.5]$ in standardized units. The emulator updates positions as $\hat{x}_{t+1} = x_t + \Delta \hat{x}_t$. Initial noise $y_K \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \mathbf{I})$ is freshly sampled at each autoregressive step $t \to t+1$.

Deterministic DDIM sampling \cite{song2020denoising} removes the stochastic term $\sigma_k \mathbf{z}$ by setting $\eta = 0$:
\begin{equation}
    y_{k-1} = \sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_{k-1}}\hat{y}_0 + \sqrt{1 - \bar{\alpha}_{k-1}}\,\epsilon_\theta(y_k, k, x_t),
\end{equation}
where $\hat{y}_0$ is the clamped estimate of $y_0$. Deterministic DDIM sampling initializes from fresh noise $y_K \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \mathbf{I})$ at each step. This rule eliminates trajectory dispersion from per-step stochastic noise injection.

\subsection{Diagnostic Probing Protocol}

Activations $h_l(x_t)$ are captured during forward inference sampling conditioned solely on state $x_t$. Ground truth displacements $\Delta x_t^{\mathrm{GT}}$ are withheld during activation extraction to avoid label leakage.

Linear Ridge regression probes with cross-validated regularization ($\alpha \in [10^{-4}, 10^4]$) decode physical targets:
\begin{equation}
    y_{\mathrm{phys}} \in \left\{ \mathbf{p}, \mathbf{v}, E_{\mathrm{total}}, \|\vec{L}\| \right\}.
\end{equation}
Identical networks with frozen random weights serve as feature controls. We introduce positive linear controls (particle coordinates and velocities) alongside nonlinear invariants (energy and angular momentum).

Probes trained on $h_l$ predict single-step rollout error magnitude $e_{\mathrm{actual}} = \|\Delta \hat{x}_{t+1} - \Delta x_{t+1}^{\mathrm{GT}}\|_2$ and its logarithm $\log e_{\mathrm{actual}}$. Reference baselines evaluate extrinsic state difficulty:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Physical Feature Baseline:} Linear probe using geometric features ($r_{\min}/r_s$, mean speed $\bar{v}$, maximum speed $v_{\max}$, angular momentum $\|\vec{L}\|$, and ISCO distance $|r_{\min} - r_{\mathrm{ISCO}}|$).
    \item \textbf{Internal Activation Baseline:} Linear probe on pre-head activations $h_{\mathrm{pre\_head}}$.
    \item \textbf{Combined State and Activation Probe:} Linear probe combining physical features and internal activations to measure incremental predictive power.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Experimental Results}

\subsection{Experimental Configuration}
The evaluation suite contains 275 independent Barnes-Hut trajectories of 60 timesteps ($N=16$ bodies, state dimension $D=102$, timestep $\Delta t = 0.01$). Trajectories are split strictly by trajectory ID. We assign 15 trajectories to multi-step rollouts and 10 trajectories to single-step diagnostics. Training utilizes up to 250 trajectories (14,750 transitions). Initial particle radii satisfy $r_{\min} = 2.5 \approx 4.5 r_s$. This places orbits outside the ISCO ($3.0 r_s$) with low numerical energy drift ($<0.08\%$). Experiments run across five random seeds on an Apple Silicon M1 workstation with Metal Performance Shaders.

\subsection{Capacity Scaling, Rollout Dynamics, and Baselines}
We train four denoiser widths on baseline data: $d=32$ ($31\text{k}$ parameters), $d=64$ ($66\text{k}$ parameters), $d=128$ ($174\text{k}$ parameters), and $d=256$ ($536\text{k}$ parameters). We evaluate matched deterministic MLPs, a first-order kinematic baseline ($\Delta \mathbf{p} = \mathbf{v} \Delta t, \Delta \mathbf{v} = 0$), and a zero-motion identity baseline ($\Delta x = 0$) on the dedicated 15-trajectory held-out set. Table~\ref{tab:width_scaling} and Figure~\ref{fig:width_scaling} present the findings.

Single-step validation loss decreases monotonically as width increases, dropping from $0.923 \pm 0.012$ at $d=32$ to $0.660 \pm 0.036$ at $d=256$. Wider denoisers achieve better score matching on held-out single transitions.

Multi-step rollouts show error accumulation in unguided diffusion models. Mean trajectory root mean square errors range between $11.36 \pm 0.30$ and $14.89 \pm 0.45$. Step errors accumulate monotonically along the rollout horizon. Table~\ref{tab:rollout_curves} shows that for $d=128$, rollout error grows from $4.18$ at step 5 to $31.84$ at step 30, averaging $14.89$ across steps. The zero-motion identity baseline grows from $1.17$ at step 5 to $5.52$ at step 30, averaging $2.94$.

Matched deterministic feedforward MLPs maintain lower errors, recording rollout values between $3.14 \pm 0.11$ ($d=256$) and $3.74 \pm 0.12$ ($d=64$). The kinematic baseline achieves a mean rollout error of $2.86$ ($95\%$ CI: $[2.48, 3.22]$), and the zero-motion identity baseline achieves $2.94$ ($95\%$ CI: $[2.56, 3.29]$). Both non-parametric baselines outperform all trained models over 30 simulation steps on the small-data benchmark.

Physical conservation metrics explain this performance gap. Ground truth trajectories preserve total energy to within $0.0013\%$ and angular momentum to within $0.0001\%$. Over 30 steps, the kinematic baseline drifts by $47.28\%$ in energy from linear motion, while angular momentum is conserved ($0.0000\%$). Deterministic MLPs drift by $47.40\%$ in energy and $7.50\%$ in angular momentum. Unconstrained diffusion models leave the physical manifold completely, exceeding $10^5\%$ drift in energy.

Euclidean root mean square error rewards predictions that stay near the central orbital scale rather than physically faithful trajectories. The linear kinematic baseline maintains lower root mean square error than learned models. It achieves this despite suffering $47.28\%$ energy drift over 30 steps.

\subsection{Physical Invariant Decodability Across Model Widths}
Linear Ridge probes evaluate whether hidden representations decode physical invariants during inference without ground truth leakage. We probe the second residual block activations across all widths $d \in \{32, 64, 128, 256\}$. Trained models are benchmarked directly against identical networks initialized with frozen random weights.

Figure~\ref{fig:width_scaling} (bottom panels) displays probe decodability across widths. Linear probes yield negative test $R^2$ scores across all models. Total energy reaches $-0.049$ at $d=32$, $-0.225$ at $d=64$, $-0.345$ at $d=128$, and $-0.311$ at $d=256$. Angular momentum norm reaches $-0.357$ at $d=32$, $-0.636$ at $d=64$, $-1.239$ at $d=128$, and $-2.082$ at $d=256$. Random-weight networks yield matching negative scores ($-0.157$ for energy and $-0.866$ for angular momentum at $d=32$, reaching $-0.706$ and $-2.180$ at $d=256$).

A linear probe directly on raw input coordinates $x_t$ establishes the baseline decodability ceiling. Exact raw state coordinates yield $R^2 = -1.234$ for body positions, $R^2 = -6.450$ for body velocities, $R^2 = -3.386$ for total energy, and $R^2 = -7.998$ for angular momentum norm. Trajectories possess distinct initial phases and orbital parameters. Linear Ridge probes overfit within-trajectory collinearities and fail to generalize across held-out trajectories. Quadratic coordinate features ($x_t, x_t^2$) yield $R^2 = -0.585$ for energy and $+0.154$ for angular momentum.

These controls demonstrate that negative activation probe scores reflect target nonlinearity and inter-trajectory variance rather than model representation failure. Linear probing cannot determine whether internal representations encode physical invariants.

\subsection{Controlled Runtime Intervention Analysis}
We evaluate an active runtime monitor using a linear probe on $h_{\mathrm{pre\_head}}$ applied to the flagship $d=128$ model across five random seeds. The emulator damps step size ($\Delta \hat{x}_t \leftarrow 0.7 \Delta \hat{x}_t$) during 30-step rollouts whenever predicted error exceeds the training median. We benchmark this against three controls: constant damping (factor $0.7$ at every step), random damping (factor $0.7$ with $50\%$ probability), and unconstrained rollout.

Unconstrained diffusion rollouts record an error of $14.13 \pm 0.10$. Probe-guided damping reduces rollout error to $10.28 \pm 0.38$. Constant damping achieves the lowest error among learned policies at $9.88 \pm 0.63$. Random damping yields $12.34 \pm 0.45$. The identity baseline achieves $2.94$.

Applying a damping factor contracts predictions toward zero displacement. This shrinkage reduces error whenever model rollouts drift outward. The comparison confirms that stabilization stems from trajectory regularization toward stationary motion. It does not reflect causal error foresight by the activation probe.

Direct measurement of step displacement ratios ($\|\Delta \hat{x}\| / \|\Delta x_{\mathrm{GT}}\|$) illuminates the sampler dynamics. Small datasets ($N=25$) exhibit severe step displacement inflation under unguided ancestral sampling ($8.21\times$ ground truth). Deterministic DDIM sampling contracts this inflation to $7.15\times$, explaining why DDIM cuts rollout error from $13.39$ to $10.23$. Averaging five stochastic samples yields $5.90\times$ inflation, confirming that gains derive from displacement shrinkage.

The gap between DDIM and sample averaging closes as training data suppresses noise magnitude. Both samplers resample initial noise $y_K \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \mathbf{I})$ at each autoregressive step.

\section{Discussion, Implications, and Limitations}

Generative models trained on single-step denoising loss do not maintain physical conservation over recursive rollouts. Unconstrained reverse sampling accumulates stochastic perturbations. Pushforward loss functions \cite{brandstetter2022message} and score refinement techniques \cite{LippeVPTB23} have been proposed to stabilize multi-step execution. Deterministic multi-layer perceptrons scale effectively with dataset volume, outperforming stationary baselines once training data reaches 14,750 transitions.

This empirical investigation provides two methodological lessons for probing scientific surrogates:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Target Leakage Prevention:} Probing activations must be collected during forward inference without conditioning on target ground truth displacements.
    \item \textbf{Control Calibration:} Diagnostic decodability requires positive linear controls and random-weight baselines to separate target geometry from learned representations.
\end{enumerate}

Three technical limitations characterize this benchmark:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{System Scale:} The physical setup uses $N=16$ bodies orbiting a single black hole, providing an interpretable testbed rather than a large astrophysical disk.
    \item \textbf{Sampling Method:} Deterministic DDIM sampling contracts rollout drift, yet recursive execution remains unconstrained by symplectic manifolds.
    \item \textbf{Intervention Policy:} The intervention experiments tested uniform scalar damping. Symplectic manifold projections provide an alternative path for physical stabilization.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}
This study evaluated capacity scaling, invariant decodability, error predictability, and rollout stability in conditional diffusion surrogates for pseudo-Newtonian $N$-body systems. Denoiser width expansion reduces single-step validation loss across five seeds ($0.923 \pm 0.012$ to $0.660 \pm 0.036$). Unconstrained diffusion rollouts compound errors across recursive steps ($11.36$ to $14.89$). Deterministic MLPs achieve lower errors ($3.14$ to $3.74$), but non-parametric kinematic ($2.86$) and identity ($2.94$) baselines outperform all learned surrogates on small datasets. Scaling data to $14,750$ transitions enables deterministic MLPs to reach $1.80 \pm 0.11$, surpassing non-parametric baselines. Deterministic DDIM sampling reduces rollout error by contracting step displacement inflation. Linear diagnostic probing fails on nonlinear invariants due to target geometry rather than model deficiency. Transition errors reflect extrinsic physical state difficulty rather than internal representations. Runtime damping interventions shrink wandering predictions toward static baselines. They do not leverage predictive probe guidance.
